# Delivery plan knowledge

Distilled from a written local SEO delivery pipeline (onboarding, audit and repair, business profile, blog engine, service pages) so `/pitch-page` can draw the plan a client sees and `/proposal` can cut it into milestones, without inventing a schedule.

## The shape of the engagement

One sprint of six to eight weeks, in six phases. Two of them (onboarding, baseline) produce no visible page and are still the phases that decide whether the rest is provable.

0. Onboarding and access
1. Baseline audit and repair
2. Search plan
3. Local profile and reviews
4. Money pages and supporting content
5. Proof and handover

The week ranges under each phase below are a working split of the six to eight weeks, not a measured number. Only the durations under "Timeframes the source names" are measured or stated by the source. All timing here is internal planning guidance; pitch pages show phases and order without a delivery timeline.

## Why this order and not another

- **Baseline before any change.** Without a dated zero measurement there is nothing to compare at the end, and the closing report has no before. This is the step people skip.
- **Repair before publish.** On a live site the rendered `<head>` often comes from template paths that bypass the SEO plugin (three of them in the documented case). Publishing new pages onto that stack means writing perfect values into a database that the served HTML never shows.
- **One search plan before any page.** Informational terms and buying terms are assigned once, up front: service pages own the buying terms, posts own the questions. Deciding per page produces two pages competing for one term, which costs rankings on both.
- **Link targets before links.** Every post links out to a money page. If the client has none, the money pages are built first; if the client already has them, posts can run first and link into the existing ones.
- **Profile work starts on day one although it lands later.** Verification is a postal code from Google and takes 10 to 14 days. Started when the phase comes up, it stalls a six week engagement for two weeks. This is the only hard date dependency in the whole plan.
- **Measurement closes, and it needs a window.** Content effects are not readable in days, so measurement is planned from the start, not added at the end.

## Phase 0: onboarding and access (before day one)

Everything here happens in one exchange, not five. One access request, one question list, one deadline.

**Lands with the client:** a written scope that names what is sold and, explicitly, what is not; a confirmed list of what you will touch and what is off limits.

**The client must supply:**

- Search Console. Without it no page can be merged, deleted or prioritized. Query data is the only source for which page sits just off page one, and keyword tools do not see the long tail below their volume floor.
- Analytics. Without it there is no baseline, no do-not-touch list and no proof at renewal.
- Keyword and audit data, either their subscription or yours. Without it topic choice is guesswork.
- CMS admin. Without it nothing can be built or fixed. Ask for the hosting account login, never SSH or SFTP: it turns a two minute handover into a week with their webmaster.
- Business profile access, and whether they are owner or manager. A manager cannot change everything.

**The client must decide:** who approves content before it goes live; who receives lead alerts and on which channel; which pages must not be touched; brand constraints or free hand; whether they come to the customer or the customer comes to them (this decides whether location pages exist at all, and reversing it later costs a redirect per page).

**The client must produce, with a deadline, or it never arrives:** real photos of the business; any recording where a customer said something good, phone video counts; a short founder video; and, if they have nothing written, a recorded self interview you transcribe into their stories and numbers.

**Blocked if missing:** no Search Console means no cleanup decisions at all; no analytics means the final report shows activity instead of gain; no photos means stock images, which is the last resort, not the default.

**Done when:** every credential has been used once by you, not just received; the current state is measured and written down with a date and a source; scope is in writing; the first audit run exists as the zero point; verification and any API access are already requested.

## Phase 1: baseline audit and repair (weeks 1 to 2)

**Lands with the client:** a site health baseline with a number, an error count and a warning count; a mobile performance baseline; a fixed list of defects with the layer each one actually lives in; a stack profile of their site (host, the one caching layer and its exact purge command, SEO plugin, theme, builder, redirect manager, the template paths that bypass the plugin, backup location, change log).

**The client must decide:** anything that means deleting. Thin pages get reported, not filled. Contact, imprint and privacy pages are short by nature and are not defects.

**Done when:** health score and mobile score are re-measured after the fixes, the property is verified, the sitemap is submitted and accepted, and the stack profile is filled in. Reference point for the health score: the best ten percent of sites sit near 92 percent.

## Phase 2: search plan (weeks 2 to 3)

**Lands with the client:** one keyword map covering both posts and money pages, with a type and a status per row, sorted by quick wins (real volume at low difficulty), hubs above their spokes; plus the page map that follows from it, at most three clicks from the home page.

**Filters, so the two lanes never collide:** posts take informational intent, 100 searches a month or more, difficulty 30 or under. Money pages take transactional intent, 30 searches a month or more, a cost per click above zero, no difficulty filter, sorted by cost per click descending. A combination with no volume does not get a page.

**The cannibalization test is objective, not a feeling:** search both terms. Three or more shared URLs in the top ten means one page. Zero or one means two pages.

**The client must decide:** which services make the most money and which they want more of; which towns they actually serve and where a job stops being worth the drive; which competitors to look at.

**Done when:** the map exists, is sorted, and every row carries a status. This is the one phase the source calls fully acceptable on its own evidence.

## Phase 3: local profile and reviews (requested day one, delivered weeks 3 to 4)

**Lands with the client:** a verified profile with a primary category, up to nine secondary ones, a volume checked service list, the service area, description, hours, attributes and products; a posting cadence running at one to two posts a week; a review request flow; consistent listings across directories.

**The client must supply:** owner access or a verifiable address, real photos, and a way to reach customers after a completed job (their own CRM or a form). Without that last one the review part stands still, and reviews are the hardest and most effective part.

**The client must decide:** the final category list. The primary category is the single strongest ranking factor and a wrong one can collapse visibility. Take it from the best ranking local competitors, then have a human confirm every category against the live autocomplete, because category lists are not published and a model will invent plausible ones.

**Done when:** verified, categories confirmed in autocomplete, services volume checked against the main city (never a suburb), at most 20 service areas, at least 10 reviews with roughly 8 new ones a month after that, rating in the 4.7 to 4.9 band, posting running without a gap, and 30 to 50 listings carrying a character identical name, address and phone.

**Say this out loud to the client, both ways:** proximity is the strongest display factor and nobody controls it. Posts do not raise visibility directly, they convert views that already exist, and three months without a post can make Google treat the business as closed.

**Caution to carry into any proposal:** routing unhappy respondents away from the public review link before they review is what Google's policy calls review gating. Enforcement ranges from removing reviews to suspending the profile. If the client wants a satisfaction survey, keep the branch in one place so it can be switched to a single link in one move.

## Phase 4: money pages and supporting content (weeks 3 to 6)

**Lands with the client:** one page per service and town combination that has proven volume, each with proof beside the claim, hours, address with an embedded map, a form and a phone number on the page itself; plus posts that answer the buying questions and link into those pages.

**The client must supply:** proof. Results with a number, named references, licenses, press, certificates. On a money page the proof decides, not the prose, and nothing goes on a page that is not in their proof file.

**The client must decide:** the build route, before the first page. Default is their existing site, because Google attaches trust to the domain, not to the technology behind it. Moving off a CMS is not the same question as moving the domain: same address, different stack, costs nothing in rankings. And within their CMS: hand built pages look better but only someone technical can change them later, page builder output is plainer but the client can edit it.

**Volume:** at least 5 combinations, start around 50, hard ceiling 400. Above that it reads as mass production.

**Publishing is paced, and this is a suspension avoidance rule, not a style choice:** one on day one, one on day two, two on day three, three on day five, four on day eight. Pushing hundreds of pages live at once is a reliable way to get penalized. For stubborn pages, request indexing individually, roughly ten requests a day.

**Done when:** the on-page checklist passes per page, reported by category; mobile performance target is 100 across all four categories; no page is more than three clicks deep; no page is without an inbound link; no two pages target the same primary term; and the clone check passes, which means reading two town pages side by side and seeing a real difference. Same text with a swapped town name is a doorway page.

## Phase 5: proof and handover (weeks 6 to 8)

**Lands with the client:** the before and after against the frozen baseline, the page map and link index as files they keep, and a named list of what was deliberately left out.

**What is honestly claimable in a short window:** orphan pages fixed, internal link density before and after, indexed pages, crawl errors, load time, form enquiries, impressions on the reworked pages measured against a control group of untouched pages. Traffic alone is not the headline in a six week window: season, algorithm updates and the client's own publishing are each larger than the effect being measured.

**The cheapest measurable win, and it touches nothing:** filter Search Console for positions four to eight with high impressions and a poor click rate. Google already ranks those pages, people just do not pick them. Rewrite the titles and measure for two to four weeks. Small, reversible, and it shows up.

## Acceptance logic: what is checked before anything counts as delivered

- **Check the served HTML, never the database.** A value can be correct in the CMS and absent on the page.
- **Purge every caching layer, refetch the live URL, confirm a cache miss.** A fix can be live while a proxy still serves the old page. Never conclude "it did not work" before a full purge.
- **Trigger a fresh audit.** The previous audit state does not move on its own.
- **Back up before every change and keep one running change log** with the original value and the way back, written as you go, not at the end.
- **Guards before any bulk rewrite, or abort:** new length at least 95 percent of the old, every original section still present in substance, any non-200 response stops the run.
- **A human picks the title and description** from two or three mechanically valid variants. This is the one place the source explicitly refuses full automation.
- **Sampling between blocks.** Unattended runs across hundreds of pages match the pattern search engines police as scaled content abuse.
- **Reversible first.** Duplicates get noindex plus canonical, never deletion without approval. Merges, deletions and URL changes are the irreversible set and happen after review, with a redirect plan written before the list exists.

## What one person can carry, and what assumes a team

The source describes a team operation. For a single freelancer:

- **Directory listings:** 8 to 12 hours of manual work is a milestone of its own or a paid tool line item. Do not bury it inside a page milestone.
- **Bulk content work:** hundreds of pages is a team assumption and a real token bill (roughly 20k tokens per rewritten post). Solo, scope a fixed block of 10 to 20 pages per milestone with a sample check between blocks, and price further blocks separately.
- **Parallel research:** the source runs one agent per source type across eight source types. Solo, run them in sequence and narrow to the four that carry the most: competitor pages, video transcripts, forums for the customer's own wording, and the client's own numbers.
- **Automation scenarios** for posting and review requests are build projects. Solo, either post manually at one to two a week or connect it to the CRM the client already pays for, and say which one you are doing.
- **Response time promises** belong to the client's staffing, not to you. You can deliver the routing, never the human who picks up.
- **Dashboards and snapshot databases** are not a solo deliverable. A dated report carrying the same numbers is, and it satisfies the same acceptance rule.

## For a pitch page

Four phases, each with the outcome in the client's own words. Add the fifth only when ongoing work is in scope.

1. **Set the starting line.** "You get a measured picture of where your site stands today, and the things that are actively costing you rankings get fixed. Everything we do later is compared against this."
2. **Decide what to rank for.** "You get one plan showing exactly which pages we build and which searches each one is meant to win, sorted so the fastest wins come first."
3. **Own the map.** "Your business profile is verified, complete and posting, your details match everywhere they appear, and reviews start coming in on their own after every job."
4. **Build the pages that sell.** "You get pages for each service in each town you serve, written in your voice with your proof on them, each one built to get the form filled in."
5. **Show what it did.** "You get the before and after against the numbers we froze on day one, plus the files and the map, so nothing depends on me still being here."

Two things belong on the diagram as client responsibilities, not as steps: access and answers before the build, and approval on content before it goes live.
